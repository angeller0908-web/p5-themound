Welcome back to the surface, explorers. Over the past 24 hours, two massive topics have completely dominated the community forums and social media discussions surrounding the upcoming launch of *The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu*. First, players are desperate to know how well it is going to run on handheld devices, specifically the Steam Deck. Second, and arguably more important for the game's overall longevity, people want to know if the progression system has enough substance to keep us playing for hundreds of hours.

As a cooperative horror title, landing a few solid jump scares with your friends during the opening hours is relatively easy. However, building a satisfying, continuous gameplay loop that makes you say "just one more run" at 3 AM is incredibly difficult. Recently, a prominent thread on the German r/zocken subreddit sparked a massive debate. The original poster asked if the game borrows heavily from the extraction shooter genre to maintain long-term motivation. Concurrently, major outlets like Steam Deck HQ have started popping up in search results regarding performance reviews. I want to look at exactly what you can expect from both the hardware compatibility side and the game's core progression loop, carefully separating confirmed details from community expectations.

## Playing on Steam Deck: Hardware Expectations

Valve's handheld PC has essentially become the ultimate litmus test for new releases. A recent nod from Steam Deck HQ suggests that hardware reviewers are already looking closely at how this eldritch nightmare performs on a 7-inch screen. While we do not have an official "Verified" checkmark from Valve just yet—which is totally normal for an unreleased game—early indicators and engine specs point toward a very solid handheld experience.

**What we anticipate for framerates:** Given the claustrophobic, underground environments of K'n-yan and Yoth—which you can read more about in our [Lovecraft lore backgrounder](lovecraft-novella-knyan-yoth-nkai.html)—the game does not appear to rely on massive, resource-heavy draw distances. This is a massive win for the Steam Deck's APU. We are anticipating that a mix of Medium and Low graphics settings, combined with aggressive upscaling like FSR (FidelityFX Super Resolution), should comfortably yield a stable 40 FPS. If you are willing to sacrifice some texture quality, 60 FPS might even be achievable in tighter corridors where the lighting engine does not have to work as hard.

**UI scaling and legibility:** One of the biggest hurdles for PC horror games transitioning to a handheld format is text legibility. Managing your sanity meter, reading expedition notes, and quickly navigating your inventory during a monster chase needs to be entirely frictionless. We strongly expect the developers to include dedicated UI scaling options, as it is practically mandatory for a comfortable handheld experience these days. 

**Battery estimates:** Assuming a locked 40 FPS and a TDP cap around 10W to 12W, you should realistically expect around 2 to 2.5 hours of continuous playtime on the original LCD Deck, and perhaps closer to 3.5 hours on the OLED model. That is plenty of time for a few harrowing descent attempts before you need to scramble for a charger.

## The Core Loop: Is It an Extraction Shooter?

Now, let's talk about the actual gameplay loop and long-term motivation. Over on Reddit, players are explicitly asking if *The Mound* features that highly addictive extraction shooter formula. They want to know if they will be making meaningful, permanent progress, or if they are just wandering aimlessly into the dark until they inevitably die, with absolutely nothing to show for it.

To set the record straight immediately: this is not a competitive PvPvE extraction shooter like *Hunt: Showdown* or *Escape from Tarkov*. You will not be fighting other player squads for loot. However, community analysis strongly suggests that it heavily borrows from the PvE extraction philosophy. Your primary objective is not simply to survive for a set amount of time; the goal is to descend, complete specific operational objectives, gather esoteric artifacts, and—most importantly—extract back to the surface alive.

If your entire squad gets wiped out in the depths of N'kai, the expectation is that you will lose whatever unregistered loot and research materials you were carrying. This high-stakes environment naturally breeds incredible tension. When you stumble upon a rare relic that could significantly upgrade your base camp, the game suddenly shifts gears. It transforms from a slow-burn horror exploration experience into a desperate, panicked scramble for the exit point. 

That "just one more run" feeling comes directly from this risk and reward friction. Do you push your squad deeper into the ruins for better materials, knowing your group's sanity is dangerously low, or do you call the extraction now to bank your current, hard-earned gains? This is the exact kind of decision-making that creates long-term motivation and keeps players logging in night after night.

## Meta-Progression and Base Upgrades

You simply cannot have a good extraction loop without a satisfying meta-progression system anchoring it. Based on community reports and standard genre conventions for this type of co-op survival game, players are heavily expecting a hub-based upgrade system. Between your terrifying descents, your team will likely return to a safe surface base camp or research staging area.

**Upgrading your loadout:** The strange resources and data you manage to extract are widely anticipated to be used for permanent upgrades. Think along the lines of securing better light sources—perhaps lanterns that consume fuel at a much slower rate or emit a wider beam—as well as improved medical supplies and specialized protective gear that passively mitigates the relentless sanity drain.

**Unlocking new biomes:** As you bank more knowledge and turn in artifacts, the game will supposedly unlock access to deeper, significantly more dangerous biomes. You are not just getting stronger with better gear; the world itself is actively opening up to you, revealing much worse horrors.

**Character traits and survival:** While specific RPG perks are not officially confirmed yet, there is a massive community expectation for a character trait system. Surviving multiple successful runs might grant your specific explorer positive traits, while suffering severe psychological trauma could result in permanent negative quirks. This means you might have to spend your hard-earned resources to cure your veteran characters, giving you a huge incentive to keep them alive rather than treating them as disposable meat shields.

## Replayability and Dynamic Scares

So, does the game eventually get monotonous after you have put twenty hours into it? A major concern raised in the community discussions is whether the scares will lose their impact and if the underground environments will become entirely predictable. 

If you have read our [Sanity system overview](sanity-system-explained.html), you already know that the game is designed to throw dynamic, targeted hallucinations at you based on your character's current mental state. This alone adds a massive layer of sheer unpredictability to every single run. Even if you have navigated a specific cavern layout before, a low sanity level means the layout could literally appear to shift, or you might start seeing deadly threats that aren't actually there. Worse yet, you might ignore real threats because you incorrectly assume they are just hallucinations.

Furthermore, the enemy AI, which we discussed in our [creatures and monsters preview](creatures-confirmed-preview.html), is reported to be highly reactive rather than heavily scripted. The entities hunting you do not just spawn in the exact same closets every time you load the map. They actively hunt, stalk, and react dynamically to your light sources and the noise you make over voice chat. 

Combined with what the community expects to be semi-procedural map generation for the connecting tunnel networks, no two descents should ever feel exactly the same. The long-term motivation does not just come from grinding currency to buy a better flashlight; it comes from mastering the hostile environment, learning to communicate efficiently under immense pressure, and constantly overcoming the sheer terror of the unknown.

## Controller Support and Accessibility

Tying this all back to the Steam Deck and handheld gaming, full controller support is practically guaranteed at this stage. Games in this specific niche almost always build their radial menus for inventory management and pinging systems with thumbsticks in mind. For those playing on a traditional PC setup, if you prefer kicking back in your chair with a DualSense or Xbox controller, you shouldn't be at a disadvantage. The gameplay prioritizes deliberate, careful movement and high situational awareness over twitch-reflex aiming, making it a perfect fit for a gamepad.
