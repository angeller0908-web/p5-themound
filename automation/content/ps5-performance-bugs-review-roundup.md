When ACE Team and Nacon dropped *The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu* onto the PlayStation 5, fans of extraction horror were ready for a fresh, terrifying experience. Setting players as 17th-century conquistadors in a Lovecraftian jungle is a bold move, stepping away from the tired sci-fi corridors and abandoned asylums we've seen a dozen times before. But as the initial reviews from outlets like Invision Game Community and widespread discussions across Reddit have shown, the reality of exploring these cursed ruins on Sony's flagship console is a bit of a mixed bag. The game delivers heavily on atmosphere and psychological dread, but it is currently bogged down by a myriad of technical issues, clunky combat, and frustrating bugs.

In this review roundup and performance analysis, we will break down exactly what you can expect if you decide to boot up *The Mound* on your PS5 today.

## A Visually Striking But Demanding Environment

Let's start with what ACE Team absolutely nailed: the visual atmosphere. The jungle environments are thick, oppressive, and dripping with Lovecraftian dread. The use of volumetric fog and dynamic lighting is spectacular on the PS5, creating an environment where every shadow feels like a lethal threat. Players and critics alike have praised the intricate design of the ancient ruins and the grotesque creature designs that blend seamlessly with the twisted, otherworldly flora. 

However, running such a dense, highly detailed environment comes at a cost. While the game targets standard console performance metrics, community reports suggest that maintaining a stable framerate can be a struggle during intense encounters. When multiple enemies are on screen, and the game's psychological horror mechanics start kicking in with visual distortions, the PS5 version can suffer from noticeable frame pacing issues. It is not entirely unplayable, but it certainly lacks the polish you would expect from a fully optimized current-gen title. If you are sensitive to framerate dips, this is something you must keep in mind before jumping into the jungle.

## The Madness System and 3D Audio

One of the absolute standout features of *The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu* is its approach to the sanity mechanic. (For a deeper explanation of how this operates, see our [sanity system guide](sanity-system-explained.html)). Instead of relying on a traditional user interface bar that depletes over time, the developers opted for environmental gaslighting. As your character descends into madness, the game simply begins to lie to you.

On the PS5, this system is significantly elevated by the console's 3D audio capabilities. You might hear the heavy, squelching footsteps of a monster directly behind you, only to turn around and find absolutely nothing. More deviously, the game will occasionally disguise your actual co-op partners as eldritch abominations. If you aren't communicating effectively with your team, you might end up wasting precious ammunition on your own healer. 

Reviewers have consistently highlighted this as the game's strongest asset. It forces a level of paranoia and verbal communication that few other co-op games manage to achieve. The Sony Pulse 3D headset, or any decent surround sound setup, makes these auditory hallucinations incredibly effective, leaving you constantly questioning what is real and what is just the curse taking hold of your mind.

## Clunky Combat and Hitbox Frustrations

Unfortunately, the brilliance of the psychological horror is often overshadowed by the physical mechanics. When you are forced to fight, *The Mound* stumbles significantly. Critics and players alike have voiced their frustration over the combat system, describing it as clunky, stiff, and largely unresponsive.

The core issue seems to revolve around hitboxes and defensive mechanics. As a conquistador, your arsenal includes period-appropriate weaponry—think heavy broadswords, pikes, and painfully slow-firing muskets. The weight of these weapons is certainly felt, perhaps too much so. Swinging a blade often leaves you locked in a lengthy animation, and the enemy hitboxes can feel incredibly inconsistent. You might visually connect with a creature's mass, only to have the game register a complete miss. 

Furthermore, the blocking mechanic is currently a major pain point across the community. It frequently fails to register in time, even if you press the button well before an attack lands. On the DualSense controller, while the haptic feedback attempts to add weight and resistance to your strikes, it doesn't make up for the fact that the underlying combat math feels off. Survival in an extraction game relies heavily on tight, reliable mechanics, and right now, the melee combat in *The Mound* feels more like a roll of the dice than a genuine test of skill.

## PS5 Technical Woes: Camera Latency and Matchmaking

Beyond the hit-or-miss combat, the PS5 version is currently wrestling with several technical gremlins that actively hinder the experience. One of the most frequently cited issues on Reddit is camera latency. There is a very slight, but perceptible, delay between moving the right thumbstick and the camera actually turning on screen. In a fast-paced shooter, this would be an immediate death sentence; in a slower survival horror game, it's a constant, nagging annoyance that makes aiming those slow-firing muskets even more difficult than it needs to be.

Matchmaking is another area where the community has reported significant friction. Given that the game's core appeal lies in its co-op mechanics and the ensuing paranoia among teammates, being unable to consistently connect with friends or find random players is a massive problem. Players have reported infinite loading screens, lobbies dropping mid-match, and frequent disconnects that result in the loss of all extracted loot. You can read more about the [expected co-op and crossplay features](co-op-crossplay-solo-explained.html) to see how the system is intended to function once these network kinks are ironed out, but for a game built around the high stakes of extraction and looting, losing your hard-earned treasure to a server error is understandably infuriating.

## The Critical Save Data Bug

Perhaps the most alarming issue currently circulating in the community is a reported save data bug specific to the PlayStation 5 platform. Multiple users across the game's subreddit and official Discord have claimed that after a hard crash to the console dashboard, their local save files were entirely corrupted. This results in a total loss of character progression, unlocked cosmetics, and painstakingly gathered inventory.

While ACE Team has not officially confirmed the exact cause of this specific bug, it is widely expected that an emergency hotfix is in the works. In the meantime, if you are actively playing on PS5, it is highly recommended to manually back up your save data to the PlayStation Plus cloud storage after every single successful extraction. Do not rely solely on the console's auto-sync feature, as a corrupted save might automatically overwrite your clean cloud backup before you realize what has happened. Disable auto-sync for *The Mound* and handle it manually until a stable patch is deployed.

## A Punishing Lack of Direction

Finally, it's worth noting the game's onboarding experience—or rather, the total lack thereof. *The Mound* throws players into the deep end of the Lovecraftian jungle with very little explanation of its core extraction loop. While some purists appreciate the old-school "figure it out yourself" approach, many reviewers have pointed out that the game could desperately benefit from a more structured tutorial or at least some basic tooltips. 

Understanding exactly how to trigger an extraction, what items are worth keeping for progression, and how to effectively manage your limited inventory requires a massive amount of trial and error. This steep learning curve, combined with the technical issues and punishing combat, makes the first few hours of the game an incredibly hostile experience. ## Final Thoughts: Wait for Patches

*The Mound: Omen of Cthulhu* is a game of immense, undeniable potential. The 17th-century setting, the genuinely terrifying creature designs, and the brilliant implementation of environmental gaslighting make it a truly unique entry in the extraction horror genre. When it works, and you are screaming at your friends because you don't know if they are human or a hallucination, it is absolute gaming magic.

However, the current state of the PS5 version makes it a tough recommendation at full price. The clunky combat, the frustrating camera latency, the unstable matchmaking, and the looming threat of the save data bug create an experience that is often scary for all the wrong reasons. If you want to see how this title currently stacks up against the competition, our [comparison with GTFO and Lethal Company](vs-gtfo-lethal-company-darktide.html) might help you decide if this is the right extraction shooter for your group.

If you are a die-hard Lovecraft fan with a high tolerance for jank, there is a lot to love hidden beneath the grime and bugs. But for the average player, it is strongly advised to wait a few weeks or months. Let ACE Team and Nacon roll out a few major performance patches, tighten up those frustrating hitboxes, and stabilize the servers. The jungle isn't going anywhere, and the eldritch horrors lurking within will be much more enjoyable to face when your controller actually responds the way you want it to.
